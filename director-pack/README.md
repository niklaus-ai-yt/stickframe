## A. Briefs that work
**Method 1, typed by me:** "Alex always wears his blue cap, and Maya wears her yellow apron. Show lots of numbers on screen. Keep it tense, but hopeful." (Worked: the cap, the numbers and the mood. Didn't: StickFrame has no apron, so Maya was told apart by her colour only.)
**Method 2, written by Claude from my answers:** "Mood: tense but hopeful. Days 1-2 tense and uneasy (dim, quiet, uncertain); from Day 5 onward warmer and hopeful; ending reflective. Music should follow this arc." (Worked very well: a mood that changes over the video gives the music and lighting somewhere to go.)
- **What if** (look Moonlight, music tense): "You (yellow, hoodie) and a neighbour (green, glasses). Start tense and dim, turn warm and hopeful in the last third. Show every big number on screen as a counter. End on the narrator's question with a slow push in."
- **History** (look Sketchbook, music epic): "One main character (red, helmet) and a crowd. Use maps and dates on screen whenever a place or year is said. Wide shots for battles, close-ups for decisions. Calm, then epic at the turning point."
- **Science explainer** (look Chalkboard, music wonder): "A curious host (blue, labcoat, glasses). Draw every idea on the board: arrows, labels and simple charts. One idea per shot. Use the lab and the classroom."
- **Money and personal finance** (look Candy Pop, music upbeat): "Sam (green, cap). Every amount appears on screen as a counter with a cash sound. Use charts for growth. Upbeat, never preachy. Close-up on Sam's face at each surprise."
- **Psychology** (look Ember, music mystery): "One person (orange, hoodie) and their thoughts as speech and thought bubbles. Use the bedroom, office and cafe. Freeze the frame on each 'aha' moment."
- **Health and body** (look Candy Pop, music playful): "A narrator character (pink, headband) and a giant brain and heart as props. Show a timer or calendar whenever time passes. Light and friendly, no scary images."
- **Mystery and true-story style** (look Moonlight, music mystery): "Detective (grey, tophat) with a magnifier. Night lighting, fog in outdoor scenes, spotlight on each clue. Keep the camera slow. Reveal the answer with a flash."

- **Space** (look Moonlight, music wonder): "Astronaut (white, helmet). Space and sky sets, stars, a rocket and the moon. Show distances and times as big numbers. Float poses whenever there's no gravity."
- **Tech and the internet** (look Candy Pop, music upbeat): "You (purple, headphones). Phones, laptops and wifi icons. Glitch effect whenever something breaks. Fast cuts, lots of on-screen numbers."

- (S14, Method 2) "Shorten the first shot, and add a close-up on my face when the card is declined."
- (Method 2, script) "Make the ending more personal. Ask the viewer what they'd miss the most."

**Camera**
- Make it a close-up of her face when she hears the news.
- Start wide on the whole street, then push in slowly on Alex.
- Split this shot in two: first the phone, then his reaction.
- Hold the last shot two seconds longer so the question lands.

**Emotion and acting**
- Make him facepalm on the word 'again'.
- She should look shocked, then laugh.
- Make the crowd cheer when the number appears.
- Have him sit on the floor, tired, for the whole sentence.

**Numbers and graphics**
- Put a big counter from 0 to 5 billion on screen when the narrator says it.
- Add a chart that goes up on 'every year'.
- Slam a DECLINED stamp on the screen on 'declined'.
- Add a stopwatch counting down from 10.

**Effects and weather**
- Add rain and a lightning flash on 'storm'.
- Freeze everything on the word 'stops'.
- Add a glitch when the screen goes dark.
- Confetti when they win.

**Pacing**
- This shot is too long. Cut it to about two seconds.
- Too much happens here. Keep only Maya and the sign.
- Use the same location as the shot before, so it doesn't jump.

## C. Tested video ideas

**Made start to finish:**
- **What if gravity stopped for five seconds?**: The demo at the start of the video: 104 s, directed by logged-out Gemini in 2 parts. Big physical gags (floating cars, people, a dog) read instantly as stick figures. Five numbered seconds give the video its structure.
- **Seven Days Offline (the internet shuts down for a week)**: Method 1: free chatbot, 1 minute, 16 shots, Moonlight. Day-by-day countdowns make easy pattern breaks. Ask for numbers on screen: they were the strongest shots.
- **What If the Internet Shut Down for a Week**: Method 2: the Claude app, 1:13, 35 shots, 8 messages. A second-person story ('you wake up...') plus one recurring neighbour made it personal. Ending on a question invites comments.
- **The same internet story at 190 seconds**: Logged-out Gemini, in 3 parts, 53 shots, rendered at 1080p in 37 s. Long scripts work for free if you go part by part. Keep each part about a minute.
